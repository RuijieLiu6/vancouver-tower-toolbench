Tower Design Toolkits

Toolkit for designing a high-rise in Downtown Vancouver. Each one takes a set of City rules and shows you where your tower stands against them.

Made by Ruijie Liu and Sinnie Choi in Xun Liu's ARCH 540 studio, UBC SALA, fall 2026

Try a tool on your own scheme

Pick one and try it on your own tower for five minutes, then tell us what helped.

These are study tools for early design. They won't tell you if a tower complies, size its structure or prove it's feasible, and each one shows where its numbers come from.

We link the tools you already use and leave the decisions to you.

We mapped how a Vancouver high-rise gets designed today and where that process tends to break. The toolkit is built around those spots.

Who does what

AI helps with the wording, code does the checking, and people make the calls.

The roles and the seven decisions as a list

AI can

  • Turn what you ask into a structured request
  • Propose a revision within allowed limits
  • Draft an explanation with its sources

Code checks

  • Units, coordinate frame, source date
  • Geometry and arithmetic, recomputed
  • Every number has a citation, or it stops

People decide

  • What the brief is and what matters
  • Which option goes forward
  • What is frozen and submitted
AI cannot confer compliance · refresh City policy · supply a survey · repair a file that lost its meaning · settle values · replace City discretion

How the towers on the landing page were drawn

A site model of downtown Vancouver at 0.42 units per metre, with the eight tallest towers: Living Shangri-La 200.9 m (2010), Paradox Hotel 188 m (2016), The Butterfly 178.6 m (2024), One Burrard Place 167.6 m (2021), The Stack 162.3 m (2023), the Private Residences at Hotel Georgia 158.5 m (2012), Vancouver House 150.3 m (2019) and One Wall Centre 149.8 m (2001). Each mass is drawn from its OpenStreetMap footprint and height tags, turned to the downtown street grid: the Shangri-La's podium, shaft and crown, the Paradox's twelve turning slices, the Butterfly's lobed outline, the Hotel Georgia tower above its podium, One Wall's ellipse. The Stack is one rectangle in OSM, drawn as four shifted boxes; Vancouver House is its mapped top block, cut to a triangle at the ground. Podiums wider than a block are left out. Our building, a ghost on the site in the middle, extrudes up to 226 m when you hover it. Map data © OpenStreetMap contributors, ODbL.
